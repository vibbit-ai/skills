## EmoHooks

Read [shared boundaries](guidelines.md). Default `hooks` mode produces opening text and visual. `full` extends an adopted hook into a light complete ad; 15–20 seconds and 4–6 scenes are starting points, subordinate to user duration/count.

Use real product benefit, audience/motive, brand tone, market/language, assets, and any specified opening. Brand/audience modules are not mandatory prerequisites.

Choose a clear emotional change such as relief, pride, love, joy, belonging, or fear of missing out. A product outcome is not itself an emotion. Read the [20 strategies](#hook-strategies) when selecting a mechanism. Full exploration normally gives three different strategies with reasons; one requested hook means one.

Write natural first-person/POV language, often 1–2 short lines, with a specific information gap the ad can resolve. Adapt humor to the brand; a serious or explicitly humor-free request takes another route. Describe the opening visual in 2–3 concrete sentences: who, where, action, camera, and contrast.

For this UGC emotional route, real settings, warmth, people in action, and phone-camera feel can work as presets. Perceptible movement in the first roughly half-second is a design aim, not proven performance. Avoid spending the opening on a title card or static discount/pack shot when it conflicts with the chosen route.

This route normally delays prominent packaging/logo. An explicit product-first request, Hero ad, or product keyframe has different needs. Do not erase genuine source branding.

For compact English copy, 3–7 words and roughly 25 characters are useful starting points, not universal language limits. Avoid unsupported superlatives, customer counts, or urgency claims.

Output `hook_id`, optional `motive_id`, `emotion`/`emotion_rationale`, `strategy`/`strategy_rationale`, `hook_text`, and `hook_visual`. Candidates should differ in emotional or expressive mechanism.

Full mode adds scenes and a conclusion that resolves the opening and fits the intended action. For detailed boards, pass the selected hook to [VB-04](storyboarder.md#storyboarder) without generating all 5V variants. Prompt-only requests end here; actual production uses [product video](../../workflows/product-video.md) or another capability.

## Twenty emotional hook strategies

Read when choosing or replacing a strategy for [EmoHooks](#emotional-hooks). Choose the emotion first, then a mechanism that makes it visible. A complete three-hook exploration uses distinct strategies; user count and brand tone take priority.

1. **Dramatisation**: exaggerate a feature or benefit to a comic scale.
2. **Personification**: give objects human speech, judgments, or demands.
3. **Unexpected Twist**: a familiar setup followed by a surprising comic reframing.
4. **Situational Comedy**: a relatable everyday situation with heightened humor.
5. **Overly Serious Tone**: epic or formal treatment of an ordinary product.
6. **Visual Metaphors**: a visible metaphor connected to the benefit.
7. **Visual Puns**: product-relevant visual wordplay.
8. **Role Reversal**: humorous swaps, such as a person acting like a pet.
9. **Slapstick Comedy**: exaggerated physical clumsiness while using or pursuing a product.
10. **Character Comedy**: an eccentric or flawed character creates the comic moment.
11. **Absurd Situations**: a clearly unrealistic setting highlights a feature.
12. **Breaking the Fourth Wall**: a character addresses the viewer and jokes about the ad.
13. **Overzealous Users**: a comically excessive attachment to the product.
14. **Mockumentary**: fictional documentary-style commentary and comic “experts.”
15. **Problem Solving**: a chaotic or funny situation leads to a demonstrated benefit.
16. **Social Media Humour**: jokes about relevant trends, influencers, or online culture.
17. **Awkward Moments**: uncomfortable interactions caused or resolved by the product.
18. **Back in the Days**: a clearly comic historical version of using the product.
19. **Over-the-Top Jingle**: deliberately cheesy, memorable musical humor.
20. **Inanimate Object Reactions**: objects react with jealousy, admiration, or surprise.

Check trend/meme relevance to the target market. Fictional experts, historical exaggeration, and absurdity are creative devices, not real endorsements, history, or product proof. Keep their fictional/exaggerated nature understandable.
