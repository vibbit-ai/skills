# Twenty emotional hook strategies

Read when choosing or replacing a strategy for [EmoHooks](../emotional-hooks.md). Choose the emotion first, then a mechanism that makes it visible. A complete three-hook exploration uses distinct strategies; user count and brand tone take priority.

1. **Dramatisation**: exaggerate a feature or benefit to a comic scale.
2. **Personification**: give objects human speech, judgments, or demands.
3. **Unexpected Twist**: a familiar setup followed by a surprising comic reframing.
4. **Situational Comedy**: a relatable everyday situation with heightened humor.
5. **Overly Serious Tone**: epic or formal treatment of an ordinary product.
6. **Visual Metaphors**: a visible metaphor connected to the benefit.
7. **Visual Puns**: product-relevant visual wordplay.
8. **Role Reversal**: humorous swaps, such as a person acting like a pet.
9. **Slapstick Comedy**: exaggerated physical clumsiness while using or pursuing a product.
10. **Character Comedy**: an eccentric or flawed character creates the comic moment.
11. **Absurd Situations**: a clearly unrealistic setting highlights a feature.
12. **Breaking the Fourth Wall**: a character addresses the viewer and jokes about the ad.
13. **Overzealous Users**: a comically excessive attachment to the product.
14. **Mockumentary**: fictional documentary-style commentary and comic “experts.”
15. **Problem Solving**: a chaotic or funny situation leads to a demonstrated benefit.
16. **Social Media Humour**: jokes about relevant trends, influencers, or online culture.
17. **Awkward Moments**: uncomfortable interactions caused or resolved by the product.
18. **Back in the Days**: a clearly comic historical version of using the product.
19. **Over-the-Top Jingle**: deliberately cheesy, memorable musical humor.
20. **Inanimate Object Reactions**: objects react with jealousy, admiration, or surprise.

Check trend/meme relevance to the target market. Fictional experts, historical exaggeration, and absurdity are creative devices, not real endorsements, history, or product proof. Keep their fictional/exaggerated nature understandable.
